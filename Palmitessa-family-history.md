**Reported DOB:** 29 July 1992 (used for record matching; no civil birth certificate was opened)  
**Research date:** 1 September 2026; family-account update 13 September 2026  
**Scope:** Public genealogy, plus a family account from the subject for his own parents and paternal grandparents. ## Narrative: Italy to Dom

Palmitessa is a rare Italian surname with its heaviest modern concentration in **Monopoli (provincia di Bari / Città metropolitana di Bari, Puglia)**. Surname-geography sources place it 13th in Monopoli; an 18th-century palazzo in the town still bears the name. That does **not** by itself prove any one immigrant’s birthplace.

For the U.S. line that actually reaches Indiana — and is the only Palmitessa cluster with a **documented** path toward Dom — the immigrant couple is:

- **Paolo Domenico “Paul” Palmitessa** (Find A Grave: born 18 May 1879, died 1 March 1962; buried Resurrection Catholic Cemetery, Mendota Heights, Dakota County, Minnesota)
- **Theresa / Teresa Firenza Ippolito Palmitessa** (Find A Grave: born 18 Aug 1889, died 3 Feb 1976; same cemetery)

Their daughter Frances’s 2014 obituary names her parents **Paolo Dominic and Teresa Firenza (Ippolito) Palmitessa**, says Frances was born in St. Paul on 12 November 1918, was **fourth of six children**, and worked at her father’s **Roma Grocery on Seven Corners** in St. Paul. Their son **Joseph F. Palmitessa**’s 2007 obituary states he was **born 4 August 1914 in Monopoli, Italy** (Find A Grave gives 2 August 1914 — a one-record discrepancy). That is the **only opened source that names a comune of origin for a person in this line**.

Joseph married **Ida Marie Held** on 21 November 1940 (her Find A Grave memorial / funeral-home text). They lived in St. Paul, moved to Grundy Center, Iowa, in 1961, and **moved to Indiana in 1966**, later returning to Minnesota via North Carolina. That Indiana stay is the first documented Palmitessa footprint in the state that later includes Dom.

Joseph and Ida’s children, from their obituaries: Rita (Dallas Dixon); Jerry (Jan); Sandi (Neal Hostetler); John (Sue); Joseph/Joe (Sher / Debra); Mary (Chris Palmitessa-Nord); and Joann (d. 2004). Joseph’s 2007 obituary also lists a grandson named **Paolo**.

**Family account (subject, 13 September 2026):** Dom’s Palmitessa father is **Poaolo Dominic Palmitessa**, born **1970** to **Jerry Palmitessa** and **Susie Shez**. He was married to Dom’s mother **Amy**. That places Jerry — Joseph and Ida’s son — as Dom’s paternal grandfather, and makes Dom a **great-grandson** of Joseph F., not a grandson. The grandson **Paolo** in Joseph’s 2007 list is then the natural published match for the father, not for Dom.

Independently, a 2006 Plymouth, Indiana, obituary for veterinarian **Ned W. Rudd, D.V.M.** lists among his grandchildren **Poaolo Dominic Palmitessa IV**. That is the first opened source that uses the IV styling and the unusual given-name spelling **Poaolo**. Ned’s children include **Amy R. (Roger / later Mindy) Burch** and **Angela R. Rudd**. **Family account:** Amy is Dom’s mother; Angela is a maternal aunt. The obituaries never caption “son of Amy” or “son of Angela.” A 2008–09 IHSAA swimming result lists **Poaolo Palmitessa**, sophomore, Plymouth High School — consistent with a July 1992 birth. A 2012-era Defiance, Ohio, address was supplied as a historical footprint; **no genealogical item opened for this brief independently confirmed that occupancy**.

**What is not yet proven in public records:** a civil or church birth for Paolo Domenico in Monopoli; a ship, year, and port of arrival; naturalization papers. The paternal line, by family account, is Paolo → Joseph → Jerry → Poaolo → Poaolo IV. Only three of those men have the given name Paolo/Poaolo. Those gaps are spelled out below.

Other U.S. Palmitessa clusters (Scranton / Lackawanna County, Pennsylvania; Biddeford–Dover, Maine/New Hampshire; Hudson County, New Jersey; Rochester, New York; Reading, Pennsylvania) are real and documented in the cluster section. **No record opened here ties any of them to Dom.** A 2016 user note on a compiled SSDI page claiming that Paolo (b. 1886, d. 1975 Florida) and Jiacomo (d. 1975 Dover, NH) were brothers remains **unproven**.

## How the “IV” numbering actually works

**Documented:** The styling **Poaolo Dominic Palmitessa IV** appears in Ned Rudd’s 2006 Plymouth obituary. The given name is spelled **Poaolo** there, matching the subject’s unusual spelling, and **Poaolo** again in the 2008–09 Plymouth High School swim result.

**Paternal line (family account + public records):**

| Order | Person | Given name Paolo/Poaolo? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Paolo Domenico “Paul” Palmitessa (18 May 1879 – 1 Mar 1962) | **Yes** | Immigrant. Not labeled “I” in any record opened. |
| 2 | Joseph Frank Palmitessa (1914–2007) | No | Son of Paolo Domenico. Born Monopoli. **Not a missing Paolo.** |
| 3 | Jerry Palmitessa | No | Son of Joseph (obituaries). Father of the next (family account). No suffix. |
| 4 | Poaolo Dominic Palmitessa, b. 1970 | **Yes** | Family account. **No suffix.** Plausible published match for grandson Paolo in Joseph F.’s 2007 obituary. |
| 5 | Poaolo Dominic Palmitessa IV, b. 1992 | **Yes** | **Documented IV styling** in the Rudd obituary. |
| 6 | Paolo Dominic Palmitessa, b. 2023 | **Yes** | Family account. Son of the subject and Quinn Li Xiang (Luther) Palmitessa. **No suffix.** |

**Therefore:** IV is a **family styling on the subject**, not a count of men named Paolo/Poaolo, and not a number passed to the next generation. In this line that given name appears **four** times: the immigrant, the father, the subject, and the son. Joseph and Jerry are in the line and are not named Paolo. Daughter **Violet Ann Palmitessa (b. 2025)** is family account.

A Rev. Paul Palmitessa, born 18 January 1931, served in the Archdiocese of St. Paul and Minneapolis (ordination 1956). Parentage was **not** established in sources opened for this brief; Frances’s “six children” list does not include him. He is **not** placed on Dom’s pedigree here.

## Italian origin and records to pull next

**Comune of origin (this line):** **Monopoli, provincia di Bari, Puglia** — proven for **Joseph F. Palmitessa (b. 1914)** by his obituary. For his father Paolo Domenico, Monopoli is the **leading hypothesis**, not a civil-register fact.

**Parish:** not identified. Monopoli’s cathedral is the Cattedrale della Madonna della Madia; baptisms would be there or in another *parrocchia*. **Do not assume** without the baptism.

## Documented vs hypothesis (short)

**Documented**

- Surname Palmitessa is rare and centered on Monopoli (BA).
- Joseph F. Palmitessa was born in Monopoli in August 1914 and died in Minnesota in 2007.
- His parents were Paolo Domenico / Paolo Dominic Palmitessa (1879–1962) and Teresa Firenza Ippolito Palmitessa (1889–1976) of St. Paul.
- Their six children and Joseph+Ida’s children are named in obituaries and Find A Grave.
- Joseph and Ida moved to Indiana in 1966.
- The subject is styled **Poaolo Dominic Palmitessa IV** in Ned Rudd’s 2006 Plymouth obituary and swam for Plymouth High School as Poaolo Palmitessa in 2008–09.
- Scranton, Maine/NH, NJ, Rochester, and Reading Palmitessa families exist as separate documented clusters.

**Family account (subject, 13 September 2026)**

- Father: **Poaolo Dominic Palmitessa**, born 1970, son of Jerry Palmitessa and Susie Shez, married to Amy.
- Mother: **Amy** of the Rudd household (published in obituaries as Amy R. Burch). Angela Rudd is a maternal aunt.
- Pedigree: Paolo Domenico → Joseph F. → Jerry → Poaolo Dominic (b. 1970) → Dom (b. 1992).

**Hypothesis (labeled as such)**

- Paolo Domenico (1879) was himself born in Monopoli.
- The IV styling counts something other than men of that given name.
- The father is the grandson **Paolo** in Joseph F.’s 2007 obituary.
- Arrival via Ellis Island / New York, 1914–1918.
- Any blood tie from Dom to Scranton Dominic, Florida Paolo (1886), or Jiacomo of Dover.

**Not found / not invented**

- Ship, port, exact immigration year, naturalization.
- Italian civil or parish record images.
- A fourth man named Paolo/Poaolo in this paternal line through the subject (there are three: immigrant, father, subject).
- A suffix on the father. His name is Poaolo Dominic Palmitessa, with no I/II/III.
- Identity of David M. Palmitessa (1970–2002) relative to this nuclear family, other than “not the father.”
